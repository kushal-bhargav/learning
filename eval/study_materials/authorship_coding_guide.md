# Authorship Coding Guide

Use this guide after collecting open-ended responses to the question:

"Describe, in your own words, who or what made the creative decisions in this gift."

The goal is not to force every response into one label. Code recurring themes, then report 2 to 4 themes with short examples.

## Suggested Theme Codes

### human_led

Participant describes themselves as the primary creative decision-maker.

Indicators:

- "I chose"
- "It came from me"
- "The system followed my direction"

### ai_led

Participant describes the system as the primary creative decision-maker.

Indicators:

- "The AI decided"
- "It generated everything"
- "I just watched"

### shared_authorship

Participant describes creative responsibility as distributed between themselves and the system.

Indicators:

- "I guided it"
- "We both contributed"
- "The AI suggested and I shaped it"

### system_as_tool

Participant frames the AI as an instrument or assistant rather than an author.

Indicators:

- "It was a tool"
- "It helped me express the idea"
- "It gave options"

### uncertainty_or_ambivalence

Participant is unsure how to assign authorship or expresses mixed feelings.

Indicators:

- "I am not sure"
- "It depends"
- "Some parts felt mine and some did not"

## Coding Procedure

1. Two researchers independently read all responses.
2. Each response can receive more than one code.
3. Compare disagreements and resolve through discussion.
4. Report the final code frequencies by condition.
5. Include 1 to 2 short anonymized quotes per major theme when writing the paper.

## Reporting Reminder

Do not overstate qualitative results. Use them to explain how participants experienced agency, not as proof of broad population effects.
